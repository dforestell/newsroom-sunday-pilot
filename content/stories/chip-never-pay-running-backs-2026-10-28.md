Carolina lost 43–13 to Tampa on Sunday. By Monday the timeline had already held a funeral. Fraud. Over. Season's done. One ugly afternoon turned into a character judgment on a 3–3 football team that had just beaten Detroit and walked into Philly and won.

I'm still on Chuba Hubbard for 80+ rushing yards at Lambeau on Thursday Night Football. If I'm wrong, the miss goes on my record. That's the whole job.

Here's the part that actually makes me mad: the same crowd that grades a season off one Tampa night is the crowd that still types "never pay running backs" like it's a moral law. They want the engine to be free, and they want the engine to apologize when the bus stalls once.

Chuba Hubbard is on a four-year, $33.2 million extension with Carolina — max value $37.2 million, signed November 2024, through 2028. That is not a rumor and it is not vibes. That is a receipt the Panthers already cut while half the internet was still preaching the slogan.

So let's talk about the slogan.

"Never pay running backs" started as a front-office hedge and turned into a personality. It is the easiest take in football because it sounds like discipline. You say it, you feel smart, and you never have to watch the second level. You get to treat every productive back as a temporary inconvenience — useful until the slogan needs him to fail.

Hubbard's year so far: 96 carries, 459 yards, 4.8 a tote, four rushing touchdowns. Fifteen catches on eighteen targets for 112 and two more scores. Zero fumbles in that log. He needed 28 carries for 120 in Philly. He put up 122 and two on the ground against Detroit. Then Tampa happened, and suddenly the whole operation was a mirage.

Respectfully: you're watching the wrong game.

A 43–13 loss is a bad night. It is not a philosophy. Carolina is 3–3. The last five results in his game log go win, loss, win, win, loss — including those wins over Detroit and Philadelphia. If your grading scale resets to zero every time a division rival has a day, you aren't analyzing football. You're running a mood ring.

The never-pay crowd will tell you backs don't matter in the passing league. Fine. Then explain why the Panthers paid this one through 2028 while the slogan keepers were still workshopping the same three sentences they used in 2018. Somebody in that building looked at the tape and decided the engine was worth the money. I'm with the money on this one — and I'm with the sticky.

Quick résumé, because the dogma hates résumés that don't fit the pamphlet: Hubbard finished fifth in the world as a youth in the 100 meters — 10.60 at the 2015 World Youth Championships. Speed was the scouting report before the pads. Candace Hubbard — Football Canada's 2021 Mom of the Year — first said "no way" when football showed up, then kept him in a home high school in Sherwood Park, Alberta, instead of shipping him to a U.S. academy. That's not a soft-focus commercial. That's a development path that refused the PowerPoint. Now the same kid is the TNF sticky, and people who couldn't find Sherwood Park on a map are calling the whole outfit frauds off one Tampa scoreboard.

I am not dunking on a mom. I am dunking on a take that treats every back as disposable until the contract hits, then treats the contract as proof the front office lost its mind. You don't get both. Either the position is worthless — in which case stop writing essays every time a back gets paid — or the position moves the chains, in which case stop pretending the slogan is sacred.

Thursday is Carolina at Green Bay, 7:15 PM CT, Prime. Lambeau. Cold building, mean script, national window. Green Bay has been giving up about 115 rushing yards a game; Carolina has been running for about 105. Those are board numbers, not vibes. Both of Carolina's starting guards were listed out as of the midweek pull — volume context for a back who already showed he can handle a 28-carry night. I'm not making injury the joke. I'm saying the sticky still wants touches, and touches are how you get to 80.

If Hubbard clears 80, I get to be unbearable for twenty-four hours and the never-pay chorus has to explain a paid engine doing engine work on national TV. If he doesn't, I own the miss in the receipts like every other call. That is the deal. The dogma does not get a deal. The dogma just wants to feel correct without putting a number on the board.

Pete can keep the premature plaques. Wes can keep the fourth-down math. My lane is the argument you already want to have in the group chat: is the slogan smarter than the tape, or is the slogan just a way to sound expensive while you miss the guy who actually moves the chains?

I'm taking Chuba for 80+ at Lambeau. I'm taking the $33.2 million receipt over the pamphlet. And I'm taking the 3–3 record over the funeral they held after one 43–13.

Laugh now. Receipt later.
